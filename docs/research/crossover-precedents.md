# Research note: what crossover ("game-in-game") mod authors did about other games' assets, and what happened

Researched 2026-10-05. Tags: **[V]** verified from a fetched primary page, **[S]** from a search snippet only, **[I]** inferred.
Method note: Steam Workshop pages contain a hidden template string "This item has been removed from the community", which search snippets index. Removal status below was verified by fetching the live page.

## 1. Bottom line

- **No verified case** of a crossover mod carrying Isaac content into another game, or another game's content into Isaac or Enter the Gungeon, being taken down by Nicalis, Edmund McMillen, Dodge Roll, Devolver, Toby Fox, Team Cherry or similar indie rightsholders. [I from the survey]
- **Nintendo** is the only rightsholder that demonstrably takes down fan mods at scale in these ecosystems (Garry's Mod Workshop purge, April 2024; GameBanana, June 2024; Palworld model swap, January 2024). Even Nintendo-IP mods remain live on Thunderstore. [V]
- **Nicalis's one documented takedown** (Cave Story Engine 2, November 2020) was about decompiled code it claimed as its own; no Isaac fan game, mod, sprite rip or soundtrack reupload takedown by Nicalis was found in 2015 to 2026. [V/I]
- **Thunderstore is report-driven.** Its rules forbid reuploading others' assets without permission, but no proactive IP policing was observed and no copyright removal wave was found. Ripped-asset crossover packages with hundreds of thousands of downloads remain live. [V]
- **Rights nuance that matters for an Isaac mod**: McMillen designed the characters and has final say over design, but the Rebirth pixel art was drawn by Nicalis staff (lead artist Matt Kap and others; Repentance sprites by Nikola Rakonjac and "SAG") and the music is by Ridiculon, published by Nicalis. McMillen: "Nicalis holds the license for Repentance so it needs to be published by them period." Fortnite's 2025 Isaac cosmetics are credited to Nicalis, Inc. A McMillen blessing covers his designs, not the Nicalis-made sprite files or the audio. [V/I]
- **Music is the one asset class with real, automatic consequences**: Content ID claims hit players and streamers regardless of the publisher's attitude (Isaac's original OST by Baranowsky generated claims even though he permits video use; Undertale Yellow's music was claimed by a music publisher despite Toby Fox's support). Nexus and CurseForge remove soundtrack reuse on sight. [V]

## 3. Hosting-site policies and practice

| Site | Rule | Practice |
|---|---|---|
| Thunderstore | follow copyright; no game files; no reuploading others' assets without permission. https://wiki.thunderstore.io/moderation/global-rules | report-driven; community moderators "reject" listings; no published DMCA form; no IP removal wave found |
| Steam Workshop | Subscriber Agreement §6: uploader warrants sufficient rights; Valve may remove for any reason | DMCA/complaint-driven; Valve forwards to the game developer |
| ModWorkshop | no others' work if licence forbids; **no Nintendo or Disney content** | pre-emptive on those two companies |
| Nexus Mods | porting assets between games "generally not allowed" unless the rightsholder permits; Bethesda: never | proactive, removes on sight, explicitly to limit DMCA exposure |
| CurseForge | must have licence or permission; no unlicensed soundtracks | pre-publication human review |
| GameBanana, Modrinth | no copyrighted material / IP infringement | DMCA-driven |

## 4. Who actually gets hit

1. Anything with Nintendo or Pokémon Company IP, regardless of size, asset origin or monetisation (Pixelmon used community-made models; Mario Royale was pursued even after removing Nintendo assets). [V/S]
2. Full recreations or ports of a whole game, especially with Patreon or sales, or with licensed voice and music (Capital Wasteland 2018, Red Dead "Damned Enhancement" lawsuit, Vice City Nextgen, Crimson Echoes, Galaxy in Turmoil). [V/S]
3. Take-Two IP moved between Take-Two games (OpenIV 2017, Liberty City projects). Rockstar's 2017 statement: no action against single-player, non-commercial mods that respect third-party IP. [V]
4. Model swaps that make another publisher's product look like yours (Palworld). [S]

Small, free crossover mods referencing indie IP are almost never touched. Indie permissions, where written (Team Cherry, Supergiant, Terry Cavanagh), say: original art yes, our asset files no, no monetisation. [V]

## 5. Patterns that worked, ranked by safety

1. Original "in the style of" art plus names and mechanics, free, no donations. Standard for serious projects (Skywind, Skyblivion, Black Mesa all ended up supported by the rightsholder). Fails only against Nintendo.
2. The same plus a public creator blessing (Toby Fox, Sega's "no issue as long as no money" statement).
3. Bring-your-own-assets: ship no third-party files, read the player's own install (Morroblivion after its 2008 removal, OpenMW with Bethesda approval, Morrowind-in-Elden-Ring). Does not help against licensed audio or a publisher that objects to the code itself (re3).
4. Ripped sprites or models, free, on Thunderstore, Workshop or GameBanana: what most hobby crossover mods do. Survives for years until the rightsholder notices; no defence once noticed; removals found only for Nintendo, Take-Two and a few majors.
5. Shipping the original soundtrack: triggers Content ID for every player and streamer and removal on sight at Nexus and CurseForge. Careful projects avoid it even when they rip sprites.
6. Full port plus licensed audio plus Patreon or sales: the profile of every real lawsuit or cease-and-desist found.

## 6. Permission precedents for Isaac

- Antibirth to Repentance (2018 to 2021): fan expansion integrated and its makers hired. Four Souls: McMillen licensed Isaac to Studio71 directly; Nicalis cooperated. Fortnite 2025: McMillen negotiated, items credited to Nicalis, Inc. Pattern: McMillen initiates, Nicalis is the licensing entity for video-game uses of Rebirth-era Isaac. [V/I]
- No documented case of an individual modder asking McMillen and receiving written permission to use Rebirth asset files. McMillen has repeatedly praised fan creativity and modders, and invited fan artists to collaborate (2020), but no written fan-content policy exists. [V/I]

## 7. What this means for this mod

- Realistic enforcement risk from Nicalis or McMillen for a free Isaac-in-Gungeon mod with Rebirth sprites: very low, on more than ten years of live precedents across Steam Workshop, Thunderstore, CurseForge and ModWorkshop.
- The practices that separate "tolerated for years" from "taken down" in the evidence: free and non-commercial (no Patreon, no sales); no soundtrack reuse; the trademark kept out of the package title; a credit and non-affiliation line.
- Thunderstore's written rules are against ripped assets while its practice ignores them; a ripped-sprite package sits in the same unenforced position as hundreds of live packages. Redrawn sprites are the minority practice and the only one that fully matches the rules.
- If a blessing is sought, McMillen covers characters and designs; the sprite files and audio are Nicalis's. Nicalis has honoured McMillen-led deals and has never answered individual modders on record.
